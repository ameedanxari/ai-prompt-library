# Evidence, judgment and trust

The workflow separates three things: retained facts, an assessment of their meaning, and evidence of who made that assessment. Keeping bytes with a matching digest establishes identity and freshness. It does not authenticate an operator, prove a source was understood or make an implementer's statement independent.

Evidence should identify the outcome/criterion, actual target and revision, source/configuration inputs, provider/fixture mode, command or user action, observed result and retained raw artifact. Captures identify the actual route/state and context. Record failures and missing evidence alongside successes. Never relabel old output as a new execution. Current source/target changes invalidate affected observations and judgments; preserve history.

A useful reviewer comparison brief names the question, original obligations, current target, actions and raw artifacts. Follow source meaning → current seam → actual effect/proof continuation → every affected target. Distinguish reported behavior from observed code/runtime and adopted authority. Inspect meaningful negatives and retain uncertainty/dissent. Visual work needs composed experience judgment; data/role work needs access and state consistency; headless work needs actual consumer/operation evidence. Full release reconciles original scope and cross-role/operator journeys, including obligations missing from an implementer's ledger or tests. A reference to separate real-provider or delivery work must resolve to actual linked acceptance for that effect and recovery, not just another operation using the same vendor.

Owner scope/decisions, independent judgment and runtime proof answer different questions. Permission to continue does not prove behavior. Self-authored identity fields, copied session names or a model-written receipt do not authenticate independence or an owner. A current `plan-reviewed` result answers original-source scope only; it cannot grant execution authority or product acceptance. The CLI can retain review records and expose their limits; it cannot manufacture a trusted external identity. Report provenance honestly and never treat an unauthenticated judgment as verified approval.

Structural checks detect the relationships and evidence conditions the schema represents. They cannot discover every omitted requirement, interpret legal agreements, judge visual taste or guarantee production safety. A passing plan stays planning evidence until actual behavior exists. Claims about savings or reliability require matched consumer trials with first-pass results, repairs, review/integration effort and observed usage; artifact size alone is insufficient.

## Scope review preparation and comparison

For substantial heterogeneous-source, replacement or authority work, follow the [native preparation/comparison procedure](../prompts/orchestrators/native-coordinator.md). The same distinct scope reviewer reads originals and accessible current seams before plan exposure, retaining bounded source-located notes, uncertainty, input hashes and actual context/exposure provenance. The parent supplies no private hints, audit answers, producer-selected coverage boundary or expected verdict, and does not expose notes to the producer before first submission. Notes may be corrected with a source-based reason; they are never a second requirements/decision authority, approval envelope, backlog or source-complete gate. They have no separate host receipt.

Only a dispatchable current packet starts comparison and candidate freeze. Refresh changed source/seam bytes, inspect material missed by notes or the ledger, and obtain one ordinary scope judgment with actual coverage limits; the parent retains/registers the genuine result. Unexamined material requires inspection or an explicit unresolved limit, never a waiver or claimed full-source acceptance. Simpler work can compare originals and packet directly. A resumed exposed context is not a fresh blind test; folders/read instructions provide no OS isolation. This sequence neither rewrites historical authority/receipts nor proves its own effectiveness.

Repair compares original, previous and current acceptance with affected dependencies, real proof continuations, targets and change/no-change branches. Refresh relevant current bytes and material coverage gaps, preserving prior falsifiers and dissent without ritual rereading of unchanged unrelated inputs. Notes or closed finding IDs cannot replace current-source review or supply new authority; independent preparation and verified no-change retain their actual authority limits.

Trusted adapters are explicit executable integrations selected by the caller. They export `trustedHost` and verify actual host-owned records and exact bindings. Requiring the module outside the project reduces accidental trust in generated code; it neither authenticates receipts automatically nor creates an operating-system security boundary. Missing trusted access stays pending.

A criterion may declare `releaseRole: "supporting"` when the original sources identify its evidence as a prerequisite or supporting proof rather than a delivered outcome. This is a reviewed source classification, not a producer waiver: its actual target, lifecycle, evidence, failures and applicable independent reviews remain required, and `productionRequired: true` still requires actual production proof. Only the blanket release requirement for a real provider and production composition is waived for explicit supporting criteria. Omission means `delivered`; every release must directly select an outcome with at least one delivered criterion. Changes to this classification invalidate scope review.

Scope and release reviewers must compare every supporting classification with the original obligation and verify that actual production observations cover all delivered behavior. A nominal delivered criterion cannot justify classifying the rest of the product as supporting. Structural validation cannot make that semantic judgment. A document that is itself the delivered product uses a delivered criterion and proof of the actual final artifact; prerequisite-only work does not need its own release.

The scope fingerprint covers scope relationships, not every work instruction, file list, estimate or capacity field. When reviewing implementation readiness or forecasts, retain the exact candidate snapshot and findings separately; do not claim a scope receipt authenticates later changes to those fields. Re-review affected findings when those bytes change.

For a delivered local command, SDK or document, a release may explicitly use `composition: "actual-target"`. It still needs a real provider, resolved lifecycle, fresh source/target-bound evidence, build/consumer/recovery duties and actual independent judgments. The exception is per target: production targets retain production-composition observations even in a mixed actual-target release; delivered staging/unknown targets stay blocked. Local observations stay local; `productionRequired` remains false unless the original obligation specifically requires a production environment. Explicit `productionRequired: true` always enforces real production proof, including inside an actual-target release. A production release remains the default. Source/release reviewers must reject an actual-target classification that substitutes staging or a rehearsal for required production delivery. Changing composition invalidates scope and release review; no historical receipt is rebound.

### Implementation detail review

For newly authored runs, set `policy.reviews.implementation` to include `independent`. Before execution, request `review --purpose implementation --ids W1` for the active work. A distinct actual reviewer inspects the selected work's instructions, files and estimate, linked original obligations and criteria, target lifecycle and authority, and relevant forecast assumptions. The host registers a separate current implementation judgment; the reviewer may inspect scope and detail in the same session but must return separately bound judgments. Review preparation does not require the work-executable boundary, so it can precede execution. Blocking findings or rejected, stale, missing, or untrusted judgments cannot unlock work. Correct the actual detail and obtain a new judgment; retain the earlier dissent.

Schema 3 runs with the field absent retain their historical behavior. Their scope judgments never approved implementation detail; generated views explicitly say that detail review is not configured. Adding the duty changes the scope and authorization bindings and requires current review and host authorization. Already verified work is not retroactively represented as having received implementation review.

Once configured, the supported canonical commit path does not permit removing or weakening implementation review roles for that run; create a separate run for a changed duty. This protects the supported workflow from reviving earlier receipts, but direct filesystem tampering is outside the host's non-sandbox trust boundary.

Implementation review also binds relevant source-bound milestones, scoped capacity and the estimates and prerequisite links that feed their selected forecast; unrelated scoped milestones and capacity do not stale it. Actual source review must assess assumptions and missing scope rather than accept sums as proof of feasibility.

A valid public `review` request refreshes the run's sibling generated views and identifies their current fingerprint and paths in the packet. If a destination contains a non-generated file, the command fails closed without replacing it. Review still requires the actual original sources, current contract, evidence and a distinct authenticated judgment; refreshed views are not review or authority.

For substantial source and implementation review, consult the sources-and-decisions and consumer-proof references, plus state-and-operations or visual-proof when relevant. Examine source-specific operational constraints, accepted architecture, stage-bound milestone duties and preparation producers, and identify a plausible wrong implementation the criteria would still accept. A locally blocked packet is diagnostic; source-only preparation can proceed without pretending it is a submission. Freeze the submitted binding after actual comparison dispatch, then retain the response before repair. Independent detail, consumer/design and release duties remain separate; one scope judgment cannot cover them.
